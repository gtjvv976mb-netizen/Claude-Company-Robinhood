Taking a floor

Purchase isn't open yet

The floor is reserved in the directory, but the payment path is not live: the Robinhood-edition access token has not launched. It is being built deliberately slowly, because taking money from someone is the one part of this that cannot be fixed with a redeploy.

Three failures found in review are shaping it: a payment claim that anyone can trigger cheaply can exhaust the chain lookups and block everyone from buying; one millionth of a dollar sent at the right moment can lock a floor as "reserved" forever; and a wallet that routinely approves transactions someone else composed has given away the privilege that actually matters, whether or not anyone holds its key.

How it will work

  1. You pick a vacant floor and connect an EVM wallet on Robinhood Chain (chain 4663). Nothing is held or escrowed.
  2. Your wallet sends one ERC-20 transfer of the access token, straight to the building's published treasury. The call is one you can read: transfer(treasury, amount), nothing else, built by your own wallet.
  3. The building watches the chain read-only — the token's Transfer logs — and matches your payment to the wallet that paid.
  4. The floor binds to the wallet that signed, at the database level, so one payment can only ever buy one floor.
